Every step was measured. This page shows what we built and what we learned.

01Weeks 1–2 · corpus

107 texts, four ways of writing about the climate

Every text is about one of four topics. The course asked for at least three kinds of text, kept uncleaned, so the corpus mixes polished encyclopedia prose with news, forum-style discussions full of signatures and timestamps, and legal articles. All sources allow reuse.

03Week 2 · Zipf & n-grams

A few words do most of the work

Zipf's law says the word of rank k appears about 1/k as often as the most frequent one. Our corpus follows it closely: on a log-log plot the words line up with a slope of −0.90. The very top is all grammar: de alone is 6.6 % of everything.

04Week 3 · Lab 3

Stems or lemmas?

To a computer, émission and émissions are different words. A stemmer cuts endings with rules (fast, but the result may not be a word). A lemmatizer finds the dictionary form (it needs grammar, so it is slower). Pick a word family: cells with the same color are merged into one term.

05Week 6 · POS & stop words

Grammar tags, and the danger of stop words

We evaluated four part-of-speech taggers on 10,011 words of a French treebank annotated by linguists (UD French-GSD). NLTK's tagger only knows English, which shows how language-specific these tools are.

Tagging accuracy on French

Ambiguous words spaCy gets right: des restrictions (article) vs la santé des habitants (de + les).

Half of all words are stop words

Removing them shows what the texts are about. Before: de, la, et, le, à, les. After: énergie, carbone, gaz, émission, climatique.

06Project · search

Does normalization make search better?

We wrote 14 test queries and decided by hand, before running anything, which documents were relevant. Then we compared four preprocessing choices. Lemmas give the best precision; stems find the most relevant documents overall.

Precision@5: relevant results in the top 5

Recall@10: relevant documents found in the top 10

« éoliennes en mer et parcs éoliens » went from 2 to 5 relevant results out of 5.

Lemmatization turns éoliennes → éolienne and éoliens → éolien, so the query finally matches the documents. Where it still fails: voitures never matches véhicules. That needs synonyms, not word forms.

07Project · classification

Sorting texts by topic

A linear SVM on TF-IDF features (lemmas plus word pairs) predicts the topic of each text, tested with 5-fold cross-validation: each text is classified by a model that never saw it.

Confusion matrix (rows: true topic, columns: predicted)

Most mistakes put emission texts into climate policy: a plan to cut emissions is about both.

F1 per topic

Macro F1 0.84 and accuracy 0.85, against 0.16 and 0.24 for always guessing the biggest topic. Trained on encyclopedia texts only, accuracy on news and discussions drops to 0.65.

What we would do next

The pipeline works, and the measurements show where it breaks. These are the next steps the course will give us the tools for.

Named entitiesFind ADEME, GIEC, COP21, Pékin and 2050 automatically, to filter and explain results.
Synonyms & embeddingsMatch voitures with véhicules: the main reason search fails today.
More and harder dataMore news and discussions, plus official sources like ADEME after checking their licences.
Multi-label topicsLet a text be about emissions and policy, as many of them are.
Smarter stop wordsKeep negations (ne… pas) and drop talk-page noise (article, répondre).
BM25 rankingThe formula behind real search engines, compared with plain TF-IDF.
